\begin{theorem}[Wild quadratic refinement preparation]
\label{mod:cases-wildquadratic-refinementpreparation}
Consume the exact dichotomy produced by phase preparation.  The three
all-even rows are passed literally to
\texttt{WildQuadraticHigherData.allEven}; no refinement is chosen and no
Hasse source is introduced.  In each of the seven positive-polar rows,
normalize the actual retained \texttt{PositivePolarSource}, retaining its
translation class and the equality between the chosen refinement's Hasse
function and the translated source.  Use the retained continuation to
construct the lower product source, upper coordinate certificate, refined
coefficient view, and all four phase rows without changing their actual
orientation or coordinates.

In the four class-free positive rows, the trace bounds put both lower
corrections beyond their critical lines.  In the two strict-high odd rows,
the actual retained \(\chi_F\)-source is normalized at
\(c_\chi=-\operatorname{Tr}(u^{-1})\), giving correction class \(1\), while
the \(\tau\)-correction is absent.  At the odd boundary in mixed
characteristic, the retained boundary coordinate gives the two exact
classes
\(\rho^2/(1+\rho^2)\) and \(\rho/(1+\rho^2)\), the corresponding actual
selected correction records, and the exact elementary phase required by
\texttt{WildQuadraticBoundaryCompatibility}.  In equal characteristic two
the boundary row is eliminated by the lower-break parity theorem.  These
packages produce \texttt{WildQuadraticHigherData.refined}, the object
consumed directly by the wild-quadratic main theorem.

\FormalizationContract
Never manufacture a refinement or Hasse function.  The all-even branch is
passed through q-free.  In the positive-polar branch the chosen
\(\mathfrak q\) must be tied to the actual retained source, and the
elementary stationary value and residual refinement phase must be
transported together; no translation may be discarded.  Construct the
mixed-boundary continuation only in that refined branch.  Keep the
strict-high and boundary correction proofs in this cohesive node; no new
continuation package is introduced.
\end{theorem}
