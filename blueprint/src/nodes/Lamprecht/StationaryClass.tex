\begin{lemma}[Stationary numerator class at chosen depth]\label{mod:lamprecht-stationaryclass}
Let $F$ be a nonarchimedean local field, let
$\chi:F^\times\to\mathbf C^\times$ have exact multiplicative conductor
$q=m_F(\chi)>1$, and let $\psi:F\to\mathbf C^\times$ have exact additive
conductor $n=n_F(\psi)$.  Fix $M\in\mathbf Z$ and $\Gamma\in F^\times$
with
\[
 \operatorname{ord}_F(\Gamma)=M+n,
\]
equivalently $\Gamma\mathcal O_F=\mathfrak p_F^{M+n}$.  A natural-number
depth $r$ is declared admissible when
\[
 q\leq 2r,
 \qquad r+1\leq q.
\]
Together with $q>1$, these are exactly
$\lceil q/2\rceil\leq r\leq q-1$.  In particular $r>0$ and $r\leq q$.
This is the Lean proposition
\texttt{IsLamprechtStationaryDepth $q$ $r$}; there is no stationary class
in this lemma for conductor zero or one.

The restriction of $\chi$ to $U_F^r$ first descends, using its exact
conductor data, to a homomorphism
\[
 U_F^r/U_F^q\longrightarrow\mathbf C^\times.
\]
Since $r>0$ and $q\leq2r$, the representative-free linear-range
equivalence
\[
 U_F^r/U_F^q\simeq_\times
 \operatorname{Multiplicative}
   (\mathfrak p_F^r/\mathfrak p_F^q),
 \qquad [1+x]\longmapsto[x],
\]
transports this homomorphism to the additive character
\[
 L_{\chi,r}:\mathfrak p_F^r/\mathfrak p_F^q\longrightarrow\mathbf C,
 \qquad [x]\longmapsto\chi(1+x).
\]
Both descents are quotient constructions; neither selects a representative.

Finite duality relative to the supplied pair $(\psi,\Gamma)$ gives the
additive equivalence
\[
 \mathfrak p_F^{M-q}/\mathfrak p_F^{M-r}
 \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
 \operatorname{AddChar}
   (\mathfrak p_F^r/\mathfrak p_F^q,\mathbf C),
 \qquad [c]\longmapsto
   \bigl([x]\mapsto\psi(cx/\Gamma)\bigr).
\]
Define the public stationary numerator object to be the quotient class
\[
 \operatorname{St}_{\Gamma,r}(\chi)
 :=L_{\chi,r}\text{ under the inverse of this equivalence}
 \in\mathfrak p_F^{M-q}/\mathfrak p_F^{M-r}.
\]
Thus \texttt{stationaryNumeratorClass} returns this quotient class itself;
there is no canonical $F$-valued representative and no hidden choice of one.
Its defining quotient-level equality is
\[
 \langle\operatorname{St}_{\Gamma,r}(\chi),-\rangle_{\psi,\Gamma}
 =L_{\chi,r}.
\]

For every $c\in\mathfrak p_F^{M-q}$, one has the exact characterization
\[
 [c]=\operatorname{St}_{\Gamma,r}(\chi)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \chi(1+x)=\psi(cx/\Gamma)
 \quad\text{for every }x\in\mathfrak p_F^r.
\]
Here Lean's \texttt{positiveUnitOfLattice} is the unit $1+x$; its use does
not choose a representative of the stationary class.  Consequently there
exists a unique quotient class satisfying this linearization.  The
formal predicate \texttt{IsStationaryNumeratorClass} quantifies over every
numerator representing the proposed class, and the construction satisfies
it.  In particular, if $c$ and $c'$ both represent the stationary class,
then for every $x\in\mathfrak p_F^r$,
\[
 \psi(cx/\Gamma)=\chi(1+x)=\psi(c'x/\Gamma),
\]
so the linearization is explicitly representative-independent.

Finally, every representative has the manuscript's exact valuation
\[
 \operatorname{ord}_F(c)=M-q.
\]
Indeed $c\in\mathfrak p_F^{M-q}$ by its numerator type.  If an arbitrary
representative lay in $\mathfrak p_F^{M-q+1}$, then for
$x\in\mathfrak p_F^{q-1}$ the lattice annihilator calculation would put
$cx/\Gamma$ in $\mathfrak p_F^{-n}$.  Since $r\leq q-1$, the proved
linearization would make $\chi$ trivial on $U_F^{q-1}$, contradicting the
exact conductor $q$.  Hence
$c\notin\mathfrak p_F^{M-q+1}$, and the exact lattice-shell theorem gives
the displayed order.  This argument applies directly to every
representative and does not use a pointwise-kernel converse for $\psi$.

\FormalizationContract The public stationary parameter is the certified
lattice-quotient class.  The denominator $\Gamma$, exact conductor data, and
admissible depth proof are explicit inputs.  No declaration chooses a field
representative, and exact valuation is proved uniformly for every numerator
whose quotient class is stationary.
