\begin{lemma}[Norm representatives at subcritical precision]\label{mod:ramification-normrepresentatives}
Let $K/F$ be a prime cyclic extension of nonarchimedean local fields, let
$t\in\mathbf N$ be a supplied lower break, and assume explicitly that
$f(K/F)=1$.  Fix an integral uniformizer $\pi_K\in\mathcal O_K$ and a
proof that
\[
 \operatorname{Algebra.adjoin}_{\mathcal O_F}\{\pi_K\}=\mathcal O_K.
\]
These are the same explicit inputs as in
module~\ref{mod:ramification-normfiltration}.

For $h\in\mathbf Z$, $s\in\mathbf N$, $c\in F^\times$, and
$c_1\in K^\times$, write
\[
 \operatorname{IsNR}_{h,s}(c,c_1)
\]
when all four assertions
\[
 \operatorname{ord}_F(c)=h,
 \qquad \operatorname{ord}_K(c_1)=h,
 \qquad \operatorname{ord}_F(N_{K/F}(c_1))=h,
\]
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blueprint-norm-representative-precision}
 c-N_{K/F}(c_1)\in\mathfrak p_F^{h+s}
\end{equation}
hold.  In Lean this proposition is
\texttt{IsSubcriticalNormRepresentative}.  Thus it records the exact
source and target shells, as well as the direction of the congruence; it
does not select an element of $K^\times$.

If $0\le s\le t$ and $\operatorname{ord}_F(c)=h$, then there exists
$c_1\in K^\times$ such that
\[
 \operatorname{IsNR}_{h,s}(c,c_1).
\]
Indeed, the norm-filtration isomorphism of
module~\ref{mod:ramification-normfiltration} first gives
\[
 \frac{c}{N_{K/F}(c_1)}\in U_F^t.
\]
Since $f(K/F)=1$, comparison of orders in this equality gives
\[
 \operatorname{ord}_K(c_1)
 =\operatorname{ord}_F(N_{K/F}(c_1))
 =\operatorname{ord}_F(c)=h.
\]
Multiplying the depth-$t$ congruence by the exact norm shifts its depth by
$h$, and antitonicity of lattices for $s\le t$ gives precisely
\eqref{eq:blueprint-norm-representative-precision}.  This argument also
covers $s=0$ and $t=0$ without treating a positive unit layer as though it
were present.

The exact order equalities are exported in lattice-shell form:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 c&\in\mathfrak p_F^h\setminus\mathfrak p_F^{h+1},\\
 c_1&\in\mathfrak p_K^h\setminus\mathfrak p_K^{h+1},\\
 N_{K/F}(c_1)&\in\mathfrak p_F^h\setminus\mathfrak p_F^{h+1}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Writing
\[
 Q_F(h,s)=\mathfrak p_F^h/\mathfrak p_F^{h+s},
\]
the raw lattice congruence is equivalently the honest quotient equality
\[
 [c]=[N_{K/F}(c_1)]\qquad\text{in }Q_F(h,s).
\]
The quotient constructor is used only after the containments
$h\le h+s$ and the numerator memberships have been supplied.

The quotient-class existence theorem quantifies over
$z\in Q_F(h,s)$ together with an explicitly supplied representative
$c$ satisfying
\[
 \operatorname{ord}_F(c)=h,
 \qquad [c]=z.
\]
It returns an exact norm representative of $z$.  This exact-shell
hypothesis is necessary: an arbitrary element of $Q_F(h,s)$, such as the
zero class when $s>0$, need not have a representative of order exactly
$h$.

All permitted ambiguity and precision changes are explicit.  If
$c\equiv d\pmod{\mathfrak p_F^{h+s}}$ and $c_1,d_1$ are any corresponding
norm representatives, then
\[
 N_{K/F}(c_1)-N_{K/F}(d_1)\in\mathfrak p_F^{h+s}.
\]
In particular, two choices for the same coefficient give the same element
of $Q_F(h,s)$.  The below-break injectivity of norm additionally gives the
precise source ambiguity
\[
 \frac{c_1}{d_1}\in U_K^s.
\]
This is uniqueness only in the permitted source quotient; it is neither
equality in $K^\times$ nor a canonical representative.

If $r\le s$ and $c_1$ is valid at precision $s$, then the same supplied
$c_1$ is valid at precision $r$.  The canonical projection
\[
 Q_F(h,s)\longrightarrow Q_F(h,r)
\]
sends the finer norm-class equality to the coarser coefficient-class
equality.  More strongly, one existential representative obtained at
precision $t$ works for every $s\le t$ simultaneously.  No independently
chosen representatives at two precisions are asserted to be equal.

For a finite type $I$, let $h_i\in\mathbf Z$, let $s_i\le t$, and let
$c_i\in F^\times$ have order $h_i$.  Lean proves the heterogeneous
pointwise statement
\[
 \exists (c_{1,i})_{i\in I},\quad
 \forall i\in I,\quad
 \operatorname{IsNR}_{h_i,s_i}(c_i,c_{1,i}).
\]
Each existential problem is solved separately, so the choices are genuinely
independent and hence simultaneous.  A second finite-family theorem chooses
one $c_{1,i}$ for each $i$ and reuses it at every precision $s\le t$.

Two corollaries expose exactly the forms needed by the stationary-parameter
modules.  If $\delta\in U_F^0$ and
$\operatorname{ord}_F(c)=h$, applying the theorem to $c/\delta$ gives
$c_1$ with
\[
 \operatorname{IsNR}_{h,s}(c/\delta,c_1),
 \qquad
 c-\delta N_{K/F}(c_1)\in\mathfrak p_F^{h+s}.
\]
This is the rescaled representative used in the wild quadratic case.  At
the break itself, every $c\in U_F^0$ has independent existential data
\[
 c_1\in U_K^0,\qquad \delta\in U_F^t,\qquad
 c=\delta N_{K/F}(c_1).
\]
The correction factor $\delta$ is retained.  In particular, when a later
stationary precision exceeds $t$, this statement does not promote the norm
congruence to that deeper precision.

All representative statements above are existential or concern already
supplied representatives.  There is no field-valued choice function and no
claim of canonicity.

\FormalizationContract
The theorem applies only at the exact precision $s\le t$, with denominator
lattice $\mathfrak p_F^{h+s}$ and congruence direction
$c-N_{K/F}(c_1)$.  The source element and its norm both have exact depth
$h$.  Quotient-level existence retains the hypothesis that the supplied
stationary class has an exact-depth-$h$ representative.

Finite simultaneous choices vary independently in their coefficient,
depth, and precision.  Precision compatibility reuses or projects a
supplied existential witness; it never defines one globally.  The only
choice-independence conclusions are equality in the stated target quotient
and membership of the source ratio in $U_K^s$.

The wild quadratic correction is kept as
$c=\delta N_{K/F}(c_1)$ with $\delta\in U_F^t$.  It is not discarded and is
not used to claim an exact norm representative beyond the subcritical
range.  No theorem in this module defines or asserts a canonical
field-valued representative.
\end{lemma}
