\begin{definition}[Integer-indexed fractional valuation lattices]\label{mod:localfield-lattices}
Let $F$ be a nonarchimedean local field and let
$\mathcal O_F$ be Mathlib's canonical valuation subring.  For every
$n\in\mathbf Z$, define the fractional valuation lattice
\[
  \mathfrak p_F^n
  =\{x\in F:(n:\operatorname{WithTop}(\mathbf Z))
                  \leq\operatorname{ord}_F(x)\}.
\]
In Lean this is an $\mathcal O_F$-submodule of $F$.  Its membership
criterion is the displayed inequality.  The zeroth lattice is the copy of
$\mathcal O_F$ generated by $1$ in $F$, and an element of $\mathcal O_F$
belongs to $\mathfrak p_F^1$ exactly when it belongs to the maximal ideal.
Every lattice contains a nonzero element.

The family is exactly reverse-indexed:
\[
  \mathfrak p_F^n\subseteq\mathfrak p_F^m\iff m\leq n,
  \qquad
  \mathfrak p_F^m=\mathfrak p_F^n\iff m=n,
  \qquad
  \mathfrak p_F^n\subsetneq\mathfrak p_F^m\iff m<n.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 x\in\mathfrak p_F^n\setminus\mathfrak p_F^{n+1}
 \iff \operatorname{ord}_F(x)=n.
\]
Membership is closed under addition, negation, subtraction, and finite
sums.  Multiplication adds depths, both elementwise and for Mathlib's
submodule product:
\[
 x\in\mathfrak p_F^m, y\in\mathfrak p_F^n
 \Longrightarrow xy\in\mathfrak p_F^{m+n},
 \qquad
 \mathfrak p_F^m\mathfrak p_F^n=\mathfrak p_F^{m+n}.
\]
Mathlib's submodule colon (its submodule division operation) is also exact:
\[
  (\mathfrak p_F^m:\mathfrak p_F^n)=\mathfrak p_F^{m-n},
\]
equivalently, $c\mathfrak p_F^n\subseteq\mathfrak p_F^m$ if and only if
$c\in\mathfrak p_F^{m-n}$.

If $(r:\operatorname{WithTop}(\mathbf Z))\leq\operatorname{ord}_F(a)$,
then
\[
  a\mathfrak p_F^n\subseteq\mathfrak p_F^{r+n}.
\]
If instead $\operatorname{ord}_F(a)=r$, this becomes the exact collection
of scaling and division rules
\[
 \begin{aligned}
  a\mathfrak p_F^n&=\mathfrak p_F^{r+n},&
  a^{-1}\mathfrak p_F^n&=\mathfrak p_F^{n-r},\\
  x/a\in\mathfrak p_F^n&\iff x\in\mathfrak p_F^{r+n},&
  a\mathcal O_F=\mathfrak p_F^r
    &\iff\operatorname{ord}_F(a)=r.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Consequently, for every uniformizer $\pi$ and every $n\in\mathbf Z$,
$\mathfrak p_F^n=\pi^n\mathcal O_F$.  The raw depth relation from the
valuation node is precisely lattice congruence:
\[
  \operatorname{CongruentAtDepth}_n(x,y)
  \iff x-y\in\mathfrak p_F^n.
\]

Topologically, every integer-indexed lattice is open, and the full family
$\{\mathfrak p_F^n:n\in\mathbf Z\}$ is a neighborhood basis of zero.
Consequently every neighborhood of zero contains one lattice and contains
all sufficiently deep lattices.  More generally, a filtered family of
elements that eventually lies in lattices whose integer depths tend to
infinity converges to zero.

For $m\leq n$, the deeper lattice is embedded, using the certified
containment, as a submodule of $\mathfrak p_F^m$, and
\texttt{LatticeQuotient} is the resulting additive submodule quotient
$\mathfrak p_F^m/\mathfrak p_F^n$.  Its canonical linear quotient map is
surjective.  Two numerator representatives have the same class if and only
if their difference lies in $\mathfrak p_F^n$, equivalently if they are
congruent at depth $n$; a representative has zero class if and only if it
lies in the denominator.  If $m\leq n_1\leq n_2$, replacing the deeper
denominator $\mathfrak p_F^{n_2}$ by $\mathfrak p_F^{n_1}$ gives a
surjective canonical projection which preserves numerator representatives.

Finally, the two lattice annihilator calculations used by the manuscript's
stationary pairing are made explicit.  If
$\operatorname{ord}_F(\Gamma)=M+n$, then
\[
 \begin{aligned}
  \bigl(\forall x\in\mathfrak p_F^r,
     \;cx/\Gamma\in\mathfrak p_F^{-n}\bigr)
    &\iff c\in\mathfrak p_F^{M-r},\\
  \bigl(\forall c\in\mathfrak p_F^{M-q},
     \;cx/\Gamma\in\mathfrak p_F^{-n}\bigr)
    &\iff x\in\mathfrak p_F^q.
 \end{aligned}
\]
